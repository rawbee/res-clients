## X · Qvaesita — *asked often, answered once*

**How does your pricing work?**
Three ways. One: you do the work, we watch, you pay us. Two: we do the work, you watch, you pay us. Three: neither of us does the work or watches, you pay us. Engagements start at $5,000. Most clients choose two. We admire the ones who choose three.

**Who actually does the work?**
Operators who have shipped for the biggest brands on earth, running agents they built themselves. The machine does the labor. The judgment is human. You will meet the humans on the call, because there is only a call.

**We already pay for an AI visibility dashboard.**
Condolences. Keep it if you enjoy watching. When you want the number to move, call.

**How fast will we see results?**
Answers move in days, not quarters. We have watched a page enter the answers faster than the meeting that approved it could be scheduled.

**Do you train models on our data?**
No. Your content stays in your CMS; we read it, improve it, and put it back. The machines already learned about you from the public internet. That is not a feature we sell. That is the problem we fix.

**Can we approve everything before it ships?**
Yes. Every page carries two human signatures: ours and yours. Nothing goes live because an agent felt inspired.

**Our industry is regulated and boring.**
Boring is our favorite. Boring categories have the least competition for the answers and the most money behind the questions. Regulated just means we cite harder.

**What if our website sucks?**
It doesn't really matter. The content can live anywhere. You don't even need to link to it.

**Will you pass our security review?**
We will fill out the questionnaire, sign the DPA, and survive your procurement portal. We have done enterprise onboarding before. Bring the long version.

**Was this website built by AI?**
Yes. The same way your pages will be: grounded in real sources, structured for extraction, approved line by line by humans. The machine did the labor. The judgment is human. The judgment is the product.
