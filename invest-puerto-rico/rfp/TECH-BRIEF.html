Context

The business in one picture

InvestPR is Puerto Rico's investment-attraction agency: a small nonprofit whose job is to convince companies and investors to bring operations, jobs, and capital to the island (helped by incentives like Act 60). Their "sales pipeline" doesn't sell a product — it lands investment projects, and deals take months or years. Every system in this RFP exists to serve one funnel:

1 · AttractAds, content, events, and PR put Puerto Rico in front of site selectors, executives, and investors.
2 · CaptureA prospect fills a form, downloads a report, or books a meeting. A lead record is born.
3 · Qualify & routeScoring and routing decide which Business Development director owns the lead, by sector and geography.
4 · AdvanceBD works the relationship through pipeline stages — from inquiry to active project to commitment.
5 · Land & reportJobs and capital investment land. Leadership, the board, and government stakeholders see the numbers.

Why this matters for the proposal: their KPIs are jobs created and capital landed, reported to a board and to government. When data is messy, the pain isn't abstract — it's a leadership meeting where two dashboards disagree about the pipeline, or a marketing budget that can't prove which campaigns produced real projects. Speak to that.

Scope, decoded

The seven problems they're paying to make go away

The RFP's scope section is written in vendor language. Here is each item translated into what InvestPR's team is actually experiencing, why it happens, and the outcome a winning vendor commits to. These seven map one-to-one onto the RFP's scope bullets (two maintenance bullets are merged into #6).

1 The data foundation is almost — but not — done

RFP: "Data Architecture Review & Cleanup" · Deliverable: Data Health Review & Remediation Summary

What it feels likeSomeone already did a cleanup project, but nobody fully trusts it. Three fields hold the same thing. "Puerto Rico" is spelled four ways. Filters and reports quietly miss records.
Why it happensYears of ad-hoc fields created per-campaign, imports from event lists and purchased data, and no single owner for the data dictionary. Cleanups decay without enforcement rules.
What good looks like
  • A field dictionary: every property, its owner system, its allowed values
  • Picklists enforced, redundant fields merged and retired
  • Dedupe run and kept clean by scheduled jobs
  • A before/after report proving it (see the demo below)

2 Marketing and the CRM disagree about who a lead is

RFP: "Marketing Automation & Lead Management Integration"

What it feels likeA contact is "qualified" in the marketing tool but brand-new in the CRM — or exists twice. Lead scores exist but BD ignores them. Sync errors pile up in a queue nobody watches.
Why it happensThe HubSpot↔Salesforce connector is powerful but drifts: field mappings added ad hoc, mismatched picklist values, lifecycle-stage logic defined differently on each side, no inclusion-list discipline.
What good looks like
  • Every synced field has one declared owner system
  • Lifecycle stages and lead statuses mean the same thing on both sides
  • Sync-error queue at zero, with alerts when it isn't
  • A scoring model BD helped define and actually uses

3 Ad spend can't see which leads became real projects

RFP: "Paid Media & Conversion Tracking Integration"

What it feels likeThe ad platforms report "conversions" that don't match reality. Marketing can't prove which campaigns produced actual pipeline, so budget debates run on anecdotes.
Why it happensPlatforms only see the form fill. Qualification happens weeks later, offline, in the CRM — and nothing sends that signal back. Click IDs aren't captured on forms, so the thread is cut at the front door.
What good looks like
  • Forms capture UTMs + click IDs into hidden fields
  • CRM stage changes flow back via Google Enhanced Conversions for Leads, Meta CAPI, LinkedIn CAPI
  • Bidding optimizes toward qualified projects, not form fills

4 Meetings and outreach happen off the record

RFP: "Sales Enablement & External Tool Alignment"

What it feels likeA site selector books a meeting, but it never appears on their CRM record. Two BD directors email the same prospect. "When did we last talk to them?" requires asking around.
Why it happensScheduling and outreach tools were adopted individually and never wired to the CRM — or the integration exists but logging is optional and routing is manual.
What good looks like
  • Bookings auto-create meetings on the right record, routed by sector/geography
  • Every call and email logs automatically — one activity timeline per contact
  • New tools must pass an integration checklist before adoption

5 Every workflow has three unofficial versions

RFP: "Cross-Platform Workflow Standardization"

What it feels likeLeads sit unrouted for days, or go to the wrong owner. Marketing-to-BD handoffs depend on who happens to notice. The same list upload is done differently by each person.
Why it happensAutomations were built one request at a time, across two platforms, by people who have since left. Nobody holds the full picture of what fires when.
What good looks like
  • One documented routing model: who gets what lead, and how fast
  • Automations inventoried, consolidated, and diagrammed
  • Handoffs with SLAs and visible ownership at every stage

6 There's no one to keep the machine running

RFP: "CRM Operational Maintenance & Support" + "Technical Troubleshooting" · Deliverable: Ongoing Operational Support, monthly logs

What it feels likeDashboard requests wait weeks. A sync silently fails and is discovered a month later at reporting time. User permissions are guesswork. Bulk updates are scary.
Why it happensNo dedicated ops capacity — they're hiring one CRM admin, and this RFP is the surge + specialist layer around that hire. Integrations fail quietly by default; someone has to be watching.
What good looks like
  • A named support channel with response-time commitments by severity
  • Integration monitoring with alerts — failures found in hours, not months
  • Monthly support log: what was done, what broke, what's next (an RFP deliverable — easy points)

7 Knowledge lives in people's heads

RFP: "User Training, Onboarding & Change Enablement" · Deliverables: SOPs, user manuals, training sessions

What it feels likeNew hires learn the CRM by shadowing. List uploads and record hygiene vary by person. When a workflow changes, half the team finds out by tripping over it.
Why it happensDocumentation was never a deliverable before — and generic vendor docs don't match InvestPR's actual fields and workflows.
What good looks like
  • Step-by-step manuals for the four named tasks: list uploads, lead management, activity tracking, record documentation
  • A standard onboarding path for new team members
  • Short recorded sessions + updated docs shipped with every change

The centerpiece integration

The closed attribution loop, step by step

Problem #3 deserves its own walkthrough, because it's the most valuable single fix in this RFP and the easiest to explain badly. Use the buttons to walk the journey of one lead.

A timely detail that signals expertise: Google is deprecating the legacy click-ID upload method (UploadClickConversions) on June 15, 2026 — mid-engagement — in favor of Enhanced Conversions for Leads and the Data Manager API, and Meta has folded its old Offline Events tool into the Conversions API. A proposal that mentions handling these migrations reads as current, not boilerplate. Industry benchmarks for closing this loop are commonly cited at 30–50% more qualified leads for the same spend, because bidding stops optimizing for form-fillers.

Target state

What "good" looks like when the engagement ends

Every promise in our technical approach should ladder up to one of these seven conditions. They double as acceptance criteria InvestPR can hold us to — putting them in the proposal signals confidence.

  • One source of truth. Salesforce holds the canonical record; every other tool reads from or writes to it through a documented path. No field exists twice.
  • A living data dictionary. Every property has a name, an owner system, allowed values, and a purpose — and cleanup rules run on a schedule so hygiene doesn't decay.
  • Boring, monitored syncs. The error queue sits at zero; when anything fails, an alert fires the same day. "Silently broken for a month" becomes impossible.
  • A closed attribution loop. Ad platforms learn which clicks became real projects; marketing defends its budget with CRM-verified numbers.
  • Routing nobody thinks about. Every lead reaches the right BD director within minutes, meetings and outreach log themselves, and one timeline shows every touch.
  • Dashboards leadership trusts. One agreed definition per metric, live rather than hand-assembled, matching what the board sees.
  • A team that doesn't need us. SOPs, manuals, recorded training, and an onboarding path — plus their new CRM admin fully enabled. (Counterintuitively, this wins ongoing renewals: trust, not lock-in.)

Technical questions worth asking InvestPR now

Questions must be emailed to talmodovar@investpr.org by Friday, August 14, and answers go to all bidders — so ask things whose answers help us more than competitors (we can already guess the stack; sizing questions sharpen our price).

  1. Roughly how many CRM users need access and training, and how many contact/company records does the database hold? Sizes the training plan and the cleanup effort — the two hardest lines to price blind.
  2. Is there a current backlog of known integration or sync errors, and roughly how large? Distinguishes "tune-up" from "excavation." Directly de-risks our fixed fee.
  3. What volume of routine support requests should the ongoing retainer assume per month, and what response times do you expect? Lets us commit to SLAs without padding the retainer.
  4. Is a sandbox/test environment available for the CRM, and who owns tag management on the website? Determines whether we can work safely without touching production, and whether GTM access is in scope.
  5. Will the incoming CRM Administrator hire be our day-to-day counterpart, and should knowledge transfer to that role be an explicit deliverable? Signals we've done our homework and positions us as the capability-builder, not the dependency.
  6. For attribution reporting, which outcomes matter most to leadership — qualified leads, meetings held, or landed projects? Defines which CRM stage changes we send back to the ad platforms.

Reference

Glossary for non-technical readers

CRM (Customer Relationship Management)

The database of record for every person, company, meeting, and deal. For InvestPR: Salesforce. If it isn't in the CRM, it didn't happen.

Marketing automation

The tool that sends campaigns, hosts forms and landing pages, and scores leads — HubSpot here. It's where a lead usually lives first, before syncing to the CRM.

Sync / integration

An automatic connection that keeps a record identical in two systems. Syncs fail quietly — the failed record just stops updating, which is why monitoring matters more than the initial setup.

Field / property

One labeled slot on a record — "Country," "Industry," "Lead Source." Data normalization is mostly the discipline of having exactly one field per fact, with a controlled list of allowed values.

Picklist

A dropdown of allowed values, versus a free-text box. Free text produces "PR," "Puerto Rico," and "puerto rico, usa"; a picklist produces one filterable answer.

Dedupe

Finding and merging duplicate records of the same person or company. Best done with tooling and matching rules, then kept clean by scheduled jobs — not a one-time heroic effort.

Lead scoring

Points assigned for who a lead is and what they've done, so BD works the hottest prospects first. Only useful if BD trusts and actually sorts by it.

Lifecycle stage

Where a contact sits in the journey (subscriber → lead → qualified → opportunity → customer/landed). Trouble starts when marketing and the CRM define these stages differently.

UTM parameters

Tags on a link (utm_source, utm_campaign…) that record where a visitor came from. Free attribution — if forms actually save them.

Click ID (gclid / fbclid / li_fat_id)

A unique code each ad platform appends to the URL when someone clicks an ad. Capture it at the form and you can later tell the platform "this exact click became a real project."

Offline conversion

A valuable event that happens away from the website — a lead qualified in the CRM weeks after the click. "Offline conversion tracking" means reporting that event back to the ad platform.

CAPI (Conversions API)

Meta's and LinkedIn's server-to-server channels for receiving conversion events directly from your systems instead of from browser pixels. Google's equivalents: Enhanced Conversions for Leads and the Data Manager API.

iPaaS / middleware

Glue services (Zapier, Make, Workato) that connect tools lacking a native integration. Powerful, but every zap is another thing to monitor — native connectors first.

SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)

A written, step-by-step way to do a recurring task, so the result doesn't depend on who did it. A named RFP deliverable.
